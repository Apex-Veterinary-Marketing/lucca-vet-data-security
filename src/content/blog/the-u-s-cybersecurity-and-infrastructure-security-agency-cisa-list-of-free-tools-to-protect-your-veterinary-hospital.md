One of the biggest challenges we face in veterinary medicine, is that we see our ‘IT Person’ as our security expert. Only to realize after we’ve been hit that our security posture was too weak.</p><p id="">One way we can combat this is to inform ourselves on the tools and services we should have implemented in our veterinary hospital. We DON’T need to know how to implement them. Rather what they are and why they are important. This was we can speak with out ‘IT Person’ to better understand our security posture.</p><p id="">Now for the legal jargon:</p><p id="">The list is not comprehensive and is subject to change pending future additions. CISA applies neutral principles and criteria to add items and maintains sole and unreviewable discretion over the determination of items included. CISA does not attest to the suitability or effectiveness of these services and tools for any particular use case. CISA does not endorse any commercial product or service. Any reference to specific commercial products, processes, or services by service mark, trademark, manufacturer, or otherwise, does not constitute or imply their endorsement, recommendation, or favoring by CISA.</p><p id=""><strong id="">Starting with the basics</strong></p><p id="">All veterinary hospitals should take certain foundational measures to implement a strong cybersecurity foundation:</p><ul id=""><li id=""><strong id="">Check for Risky Software on your workstations.</strong><ul id=""><li>Check the <a id="" href="https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog">CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) Catalog</a> for software used by your organization and, if listed, update the software to the latest version according to the vendor’s instructions. <strong id="">Note:</strong> CISA continually updates the KEV catalog with known exploited vulnerabilities.</li></ul></li><li id=""><strong id="">Implement multifactor authentication (MFA).</strong><ul id=""><li>Use <a id="" href="https://www.cisa.gov/mfa">multifactor authentication</a> where possible. MFA is a layered approach to securing your online accounts and the data they contain. When you enable MFA in your online services (like email), you must provide a combination of two or more authenticators to verify your identity before the service grants you access. Using MFA protects your account more than just using a username and password. Why? Because even if one factor (like your password) becomes compromised, unauthorized users will be unable to meet the second authentication requirement, ultimately stopping them from gaining access to your accounts.</li></ul></li><li id=""><strong id="">STOP </strong><a id="" href="https://www.cisa.gov/BadPractices"><strong id="">bad practices</strong></a><strong id="">.</strong><ul id=""><li>Take immediate steps to: (1) replace end-of-life software products that no longer receive software updates;</li><li>(2) replace any system or products that rely on known/default/unchangeable passwords; and</li><li>(3) adopt MFA (see above) for remote or administrative access to important systems, resources, or databases.</li></ul></li><li id=""><strong id="">Sign up for CISA’s Cyber Hygiene Vulnerability Scanning.</strong><ul id=""><li>Register for this service by emailing <a id="" href="mailto:vulnerability@cisa.dhs.gov">vulnerability@cisa.dhs.gov</a>. Once initiated, this service is mostly automated and requires little direct interaction. CISA performs the vulnerability scans and delivers a weekly report. After CISA receives the required paperwork, scanning will start within 72 hours and organizations will begin receiving reports within two weeks. <strong id="">Note: </strong>vulnerability scanning helps secure internet-facing systems from weak configurations and known vulnerabilities and encourages the adoption of best practices.</li></ul></li><li id=""><strong id="">Get your Stuff Off Search (S.O.S.).</strong><ul id=""><li>While zero-day attacks draw the most attention, frequently, less complex exposures to both cyber and physical security are missed. Get your <a id="" href="https://www.cisa.gov/publication/stuff-off-search">Stuff Off Search</a>–S.O.S.–and reduce internet attack surfaces that are visible to anyone on web-based search platforms.</li></ul></li></ul><p id=""><strong id="">Free tools you should implement today:</strong></p><p id="">The CISA site lists a whole lot of FREE tools that you can use to shore up your cyber security defenses.
